Written against the final cut (`renders/roundone-launch.mp4`) and the cue sheet in `BEATS.md`. The film runs on a 120 BPM grid: a beat every 0.5s, a bar every 2s, every frame starting on a bar line.

About 160 words over 66s (~2.4 words/sec, a relaxed promo pace). Each line has a window it must fit in, timed so the words land with the matching on-screen hit. Lines marked *(trim)* are the first to cut if a read runs long. The VO never talks over the ROUND 1 slam, and it leaves the pixel wipes (0:15.65, 0:21.65, 0:33.65, 0:39.65, 0:59.65) clear.

## Delivery notes

- **Tone:** confident and a little playful, closer to a game trailer than a SaaS explainer. The first 16s are the problem, so keep them dry and weary; lift the energy on the drop at 0:16.
- **The gaps are deliberate.** Frame 6 (0:28–0:34) is three short phrases, each landing on a headline swap with a beat of air between them. Don't fill it.
- **Pronunciation:** Nebius "NEB-ee-us", Nemotron "NEM-oh-tron", Tavily "TAV-ih-lee", roundone.dev "round one dot dev".
- **"ROUND ONE!"** works best as a separate take: shouted, then processed (pitched down with light distortion, or put through a bitcrusher) so it reads as an arcade announcer rather than the narrator. Leave it out and the slam still carries the moment.
- **Recording:** play the silent render (`renders/roundone-launch-silent.mp4`) while you read, so the lines land on the hits. If a line runs long, cut a *(trim)* line before you speed up the read.
- **Mix:** the music bus sits at -4 dB into a -1 dB limiter (`kit/add-overlays.mjs`). Under the voice it needs to duck about 8–10 dB. Once the VO is recorded, it can be mounted as its own track with the music ducking under it, then re-rendered.
