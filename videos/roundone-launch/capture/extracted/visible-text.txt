[p] The new normal
[h2] Building got cheap. Judging didn't.
[p] When a weekend team can ship a working app with a coding agent, “does it run?” stops separating the field. What separates projects now is what a person brought: the problem they picked, the insight, the taste.
[h3] Ten times the pile, same three judges
[div] Submissions multiplied. The panel didn’t. At a careful eight minutes a look, 1,000 projects is 400 judge-hours, and in practice nobody runs the code. The projects at the bottom of the pile get the tired judges.

[h3] Gates go first
[p] The agent screens every project against your must-pass rules before a judge opens one.
[h3] Daily batches
[p] Release a queue over several days instead of one wall of 200. Judges can start the next batch early.
[h3] A different order for each judge
[p] No project always lands at the tired end of the pile.
[h3] One private link, no account
[p] Judges open their link and start. It shows their queue, what they'll score and roughly how long it takes.
[h3] Your score first
[p] The agent's score stays hidden until a judge submits their own, so it can't anchor them.
[h3] Nobody sees anyone else's
[p] Judges never see each other's scores, so the loudest voice on the panel doesn't set the bar.
[p] Humans in the loop
[h2] The agent does the homework. People make the call.
[p] Judges' average scores set the ranking. The agent's score only breaks ties, and it never picks a winner.
